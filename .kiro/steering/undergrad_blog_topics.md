# Research on Nursing Undergraduates in Kenya (2026)

## 1. Key Challenges Faced by Nursing Undergrads (BScN)
Based on current research and student reports in Kenya, BScN undergrads face specific challenges that cause anxiety and affect their performance, both academically and during clinical placements:

- **The Theory-Practice Gap:** There is a significant disconnect between classroom theory and clinical reality. Standard procedures taught in universities are often impossible to replicate in crowded public hospitals due to resource constraints.
- **Burnout and Emotional Toll:** Hectic schedules, long 12-hour shifts during clinicals, and early exposure to patient suffering without adequate psychological support lead to severe burnout.
- **Clinical Environment Constraints:** Overcrowding at major referral hospitals (e.g., KNH, MTRH) used by multiple institutions simultaneously. Undergrads often feel like an "extra pair of hands" for ward chores rather than learners being mentored.
- **NCK Indexing Anxiety:** The strict 60-day window to get indexed by the Nursing Council of Kenya (NCK) after starting training creates early panic. Students who miss this are ineligible for licensure exams.
- **Curriculum Redundancy (RN to BScN):** For in-service nurses upgrading to a BScN, there's frustration over redundant learning outcomes and a lack of recognition for prior learning, leading to motivation loss.
- **Financial Strain:** The cost of tuition, clinical placement fees, accommodation near hospitals, and transport burdens many undergrads.
