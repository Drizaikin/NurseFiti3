# NurseFiti Blog Generation Guidelines

This file governs every blog post created for **nursefiti.co.ke**. It must be read and followed in full before writing any post. No exceptions.

---

## 1. Purpose of the Blog

The NurseFiti blog serves three goals in this priority order:

1. **Convert readers into NurseFiti users** — every post must have a clear path that leads the reader to sign up or engage with NurseFiti features
2. **Build trust and authority** in the Kenyan nursing exam space — NCK, KRCHN, BScN, Higher Diploma, DigiProctor
3. **Rank on Google** for high-intent nursing exam queries in Kenya

Every post must do all three. A post that educates but does not market is incomplete. A post that markets but does not educate will be ignored.

---

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary:** Kenyan nursing graduates (KRCHN, BScN, Higher Diploma) preparing for the NCK licensure exam. Age range 22–32. Mobile-first. High anxiety about exam failure. Motivated by career progression and financial independence.

**Secondary:** Nursing students in their final year approaching graduation. Nursing tutors and educators. Parents/guardians supporting students financially.

**Psychographic profile:**
- Worried about failing and waiting another cycle (6 months lost)
- Wants to pass first attempt to start earning and begin internship
- Distrusts generic study advice — responds to Kenya-specific, NCK-specific content
- Responds to peer stories ("how I passed"), authority ("NCK confirmed"), and urgency ("exam in X days")
- Shares content in WhatsApp nursing groups — write for shareability

## 3. Information Standards — Non-Negotiable

### 3.1 Accuracy & Sources
- **Every factual claim must be sourced.** No exceptions.
- Sources must be one of:
  - Official NCK website: [nckenya.com](https://www.nckenya.com)
  - NCK Online Services Portal: [osp.nckenya.go.ke](https://osp.nckenya.go.ke)
  - Kenya Ministry of Health: [health.go.ke](https://www.health.go.ke)
  - WHO Kenya / global WHO guidelines
  - Kenya Gazette / official legislation
  - Peer-reviewed academic journals (cite author, year, journal, DOI)
  - Established Kenyan news outlets (Tuko, Citizen Digital, Nation, Standard, KBC, Kahawa Tungu) — for news/announcements only
- **Propaganda and unverified claims are not acceptable.** If a claim comes from a source that may be biased (e.g. an institution defending itself), it must be clearly quoted and labelled as such: *"According to [Institution], who stated in response to NCK's warning..."*
- **Do not paraphrase source content beyond 30 consecutive words.** Summarise and link; do not reproduce.
- All inline links must go to the **original source**, not to a secondary report of it.

### 3.2 Timeliness
- Information must reflect the **current NCK examination cycle**. Do not publish exam dates, results, or registration deadlines from past cycles without clearly labelling them as historical.
- Before publishing any post with exam dates or results, verify on [nckenya.com](https://www.nckenya.com) or [osp.nckenya.go.ke](https://osp.nckenya.go.ke).
- Add a visible disclaimer on time-sensitive posts: *"Last verified: [date]. Always confirm current details at nckenya.com."*
- NCK typically runs **3 exam sittings per year**: February, May, and August/November.

### 3.3 References Section
Every post must end with a **"Sources & References"** section that:
- Lists every source cited in the post
- Includes author/organisation, title, publication date where available, and a live hyperlink
- Is visually distinct from the article body (smaller text, muted colour, ruled separator)
- Includes a disclaimer: *"Always verify current exam information directly at [nckenya.com](https://www.nckenya.com)."*

## 4. Reader Psychology & Engagement Techniques

Apply these proven techniques in every post. They are not optional — they are what make the difference between a post that gets shared and one that gets ignored.

### 4.1 The Hook (First 3 sentences)
The opening must do one of:
- **Agitate the pain**: *"Most nursing graduates fail the NCK exam not because they studied the wrong content — but because they practised the wrong way."*
- **Challenge a belief**: *"You've been told to read your notes twice. The research says that's almost useless."*
- **State a surprising fact**: *"NCK has 3 exam sittings a year. Miss one, and you wait 4–6 months to try again."*
- **Open a loop**: *"There's one thing candidates who pass first attempt do differently — and it has nothing to do with how smart they are."*

Never start with *"In this article..."* or *"This guide will teach you..."* — that is passive and loses the reader immediately.

### 4.2 Curiosity Gaps (Open Loop Technique)
Introduce a question or incomplete idea early in the post that is only resolved later. Example: *"There's a specific mistake 7 in 10 KRCHN candidates make in Paper II — we'll cover it in the revision plan section below."* This keeps readers scrolling.

### 4.3 Social Proof
Where possible, include real signals of NurseFiti's credibility:
- Number of registered students
- Number of questions in the question bank
- Candidate success stories (anonymised or with consent)
- *"Join [X] Kenyan nurses already preparing on NurseFiti"*

### 4.4 Urgency & Scarcity (Ethical Use Only)
Use real urgency — actual exam dates, real registration deadlines. Never fabricate urgency. Examples:
- *"The August 2026 sitting is approximately 8 weeks away."*
- *"NCK does not accommodate late registration — once the deadline passes, you wait for the next cycle."*

### 4.5 The PASTOR Framework (for problem-solving posts)
- **P**roblem — name the exact problem the reader has
- **A**mplify — show what happens if they don't solve it
- **S**tory/Solution — introduce NurseFiti as the solution via a story or scenario
- **T**estimony — social proof
- **O**ffer — the specific NurseFiti feature that solves it
- **R**esponse — clear CTA

### 4.6 Pattern Interrupts
Break up long sections with:
- Callout boxes (key takeaways, warnings, tips)
- Short bold statements standing alone as paragraphs
- Numbered frameworks (e.g. "The 4-Step Approach to NCK MCQs")
- Pull quotes from the content

### 4.7 The Zeigarnik Effect
Reference content that is "below" in the post to keep readers reading: *"We'll cover exactly how to use this in your revision plan — keep reading."*

### 4.8 Specificity = Credibility
Vague claims lose trust. Specific claims build it.
- ❌ *"Many students fail the exam"*
- ✅ *"NCK reports that a significant proportion of first-attempt candidates do not pass — making structured preparation, not casual revision, the distinguishing factor"*

## 5. NurseFiti Marketing Integration

### 5.1 The Rule of 3 CTAs
Every post must contain **exactly 3 calls to action**:
1. **Early CTA** (after the first major section) — subtle, low-commitment: *"Practise questions on this topic free on NurseFiti →"*
2. **Mid-post CTA** (after the core content) — feature-specific: *"NurseFiti's mock exam replicates the DigiProctor interface exactly. [Try a free mock exam →]"*
3. **End CTA** (closing banner) — high-commitment: *"Start your free NurseFiti account — no credit card required"*

### 5.2 Feature-Content Alignment
Match the post topic to the NurseFiti feature that solves it. **All CTAs from blog posts must link to `/signup` — never directly to authenticated routes like `/practice`, `/mock-exam`, `/flashcards`, or `/revision-plan`.** Unauthenticated readers must be taken to the sign-up page, not dropped inside the app.

| Post Topic | Feature to Promote | CTA Link |
|---|---|---|
| Exam prep guide | Full platform | `/signup` |
| KRCHN/BScN tips | Practice questions + revision plan | `/signup` |
| DigiProctor guide | Mock exam module | `/signup` |
| Past papers | Question bank + rationales | `/signup` |
| Spaced repetition | Flashcard module | `/signup` |
| Exam results / news | Start prep now | `/signup` |
| Tutor content | Tutor booking | `/signup` |
| Exam anxiety | Community + tutor | `/signup` |

### 5.3 Soft Selling (Not Hard Selling)
NurseFiti's audience is students under financial and academic pressure. Hard selling alienates them. Use value-first positioning:
- Demonstrate the problem clearly
- Show NurseFiti solves it with specifics (not generics)
- Make the free tier feel like enough to get started
- Let the product sell itself through feature descriptions

### 5.4 WhatsApp Shareability
End every post with a one-paragraph **"share this"** summary written as if a friend is texting it in a nursing WhatsApp group. Make it easy for readers to copy-paste and share. Example:
> *"Found this — really useful breakdown of what's in KRCHN Paper I and the best way to revise it. Also has the NCK news about the unaccredited college everyone's been talking about: nursefiti.co.ke/blog/..."*

## 6. SEO Requirements

### 6.1 Title
- 50–60 characters
- Must contain the primary keyword (e.g. "NCK exam Kenya 2026", "KRCHN revision tips", "DigiProctor NCK")
- Include the year where relevant — Kenyan students search with years
- Use a power word: *Complete, Proven, Official, Warning, Results, How to Pass*

### 6.2 Meta Description
- 140–155 characters
- Contains primary keyword + secondary keyword
- Has a verb (action): *"Learn...", "Discover...", "Find out..."*
- Does not duplicate the title

### 6.3 URL Slug
- Lowercase, hyphenated
- 3–6 words
- Contains the primary keyword
- No stop words (a, the, and, of) unless necessary
- Examples: `/blog/nck-exam-preparation-guide-kenya`, `/blog/krchn-paper-1-revision-tips`

### 6.4 Heading Structure
- One `H1` — the post title
- `H2` for major sections (5–8 per post)
- `H3` for subsections
- No skipping levels
- H2s should contain secondary keywords naturally — not stuffed

### 6.5 Keyword Strategy
- **Primary keyword**: one per post, in title + first paragraph + one H2 + meta description
- **Secondary keywords**: 3–5 per post, spread naturally through the content
- **LSI keywords** (related terms): include naturally — e.g. for "NCK exam": licensure, KRCHN, BScN, DigiProctor, nursing council, Kenya, CBT, mock exam
- **Keyword density**: 1–2% for primary keyword. Never stuff.

### 6.6 Internal Linking
Every post must link to at least **2 other NurseFiti blog posts** or app pages. Use descriptive anchor text:
- ✅ *"our guide to the DigiProctor interface"*
- ❌ *"click here"*

### 6.7 Image SEO
- Every image must have a descriptive `alt` text containing a keyword
- Use `loading="lazy"` for below-fold images, `loading="eager"` for the hero
- Images from Unsplash: use `?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop` for optimised delivery
- Add a caption crediting the source where required by the licence

### 6.8 Schema Markup
Every post auto-generates `BlogPosting` JSON-LD via the existing `articleJsonLd` in `app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`. Ensure these fields are always populated:
- `headline` — post title
- `description` — post excerpt
- `datePublished` — post date
- `image` — hero image URL
- `author` — NurseFiti organisation
- `keywords` — cadres + topic keywords

### 6.9 Content Length
- News/update posts: minimum 800 words
- Study guide posts: minimum 1,500 words
- Longer is better for SEO **only if** the content is genuinely useful — do not pad
- Use headers, bullets, and callouts to improve readability (Flesch–Kincaid score target: 60–70, accessible to Form 4 reading level)

## 7. NurseFiti Brand Standards in Blog Posts

### 7.1 Tone of Voice
- **Knowledgeable but not condescending** — speak as a senior colleague who has been through the exam system
- **Direct and honest** — no vague reassurances. Tell the reader what is actually hard and how to deal with it
- **Warm and Kenyan** — reference the Kenyan context (NCK, Nairobi, county hospitals, internship, registration) — not generic "nursing exam" content
- **Urgent without being alarmist** — convey the real stakes of the exam without causing panic

### 7.2 Brand Colors in Rendered Posts
All blog UI must use NurseFiti brand tokens only. Never use Tailwind's generic palette (blue-100, green-500, purple-700, red-400) for UI elements:

| Element | Token |
|---|---|
| Category badges | `bg-primary-light text-primary` or `bg-accent-light text-accent-dark` |
| H2 dividers | `border-primary/20` |
| Bullet dots | `bg-accent` |
| Numbered circles | `bg-primary text-white` |
| Inline links | `text-primary hover:text-primary-mid` |
| CTA banner | `bg-gradient from #08514F to #0A6865` with amber button |
| Sources section | Muted `text-neutral-mid`, smaller font, `border-primary/20` top rule |

### 7.3 Logo & Brand Mentions
- Refer to the platform as **NurseFiti** (capital N, capital F) — never "nursefiti", "Nurse Fiti", or "NF"
- Always hyperlink NurseFiti mentions to `https://www.nursefiti.co.ke`
- In CTAs, use: *"Start free on NurseFiti"* — not *"Click here"* or *"Visit our website"*

### 7.4 Hero Images
- Every post must have a hero image
- Source from Unsplash (free licence) using the CDN: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-[ID]?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop`
- Apply a teal gradient overlay: `linear-gradient(to bottom, rgba(8,81,79,0.10) 0%, rgba(8,81,79,0.45) 100%)`
- Alt text must be descriptive and contain a keyword

## 9. Technical Implementation

### Adding a New Post
1. Add the post object to `POSTS_NEWS` (for news) or `POSTS` (for evergreen guides) in `app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`
2. Add the same post metadata (no `content` field) to the `POSTS` array in `app/blog/page.tsx`
3. Add the hero image entry to `POST_IMAGES` in both files
4. Add the slug to `app/sitemap.ts` with appropriate `priority` and `changeFrequency`
5. Push to git — Vercel deploys automatically

### Embedding a Sample Practice Question (Optional, Where Relevant)
Blog posts may include **one interactive sample MCQ** to give readers a taste of the NurseFiti question experience. Use this only where it adds genuine educational value — do not force a question into every post.

**How it works:**
1. In the post `content` string, add the embed placeholder where you want the question to appear:
   ```html
   <div class="sample-mcq-embed" data-question-slug="your-unique-slug"></div>
   ```
2. In `components/blog/BlogSampleMCQ.tsx`, add the question data to the `SAMPLE_QUESTIONS` object, keyed by the slug used above.
3. The component renders an interactive MCQ card matching the exact design of NurseFiti's practice interface — option selection, submit button, correct/incorrect feedback, and rationale.
4. The "Ask NurseFiti AI" button is included but **prompts the reader to create a free account** instead of calling the API. This serves as a natural CTA.
5. The question must be **factually accurate**, sourced from the NurseFiti question bank, and relevant to the post topic.

**Rules:**
- Only include a sample question where it is **educationally relevant** to the post content
- The question must be from a topic covered in the post (e.g., a pre-eclampsia question in a midwifery post)
- Never include more than **1 sample question per post** — it is a taste, not a buffet
- Always follow the question with a CTA linking to `/signup`
- The AI prompt in the component must always redirect to `/signup` — never call the actual AI API for unauthenticated readers

### Security Rule — CTA Links
**Never link from a blog post directly to any authenticated route.** This includes `/practice`, `/mock-exam`, `/flashcards`, `/revision-plan`, `/dashboard`, `/bookings`, `/settings`, `/analytics`, `/admin`, or any tutor route. All CTAs must point to `/signup` or `/login`. Authenticated users who click `/signup` are redirected to their dashboard automatically by the auth flow.

## 10. Content Calendar Guidance

Post frequency: **minimum 1 post per week**, targeting:
- Every NCK results release (February, May, August cycles)
- Every registration deadline opening
- Any NCK public warnings or policy changes
- Evergreen study guides (these rank long-term — prioritise)
- Seasonal content: *"X weeks to the August exam — here's your plan"*

High-value evergreen topics not yet covered:
- NCK exam anxiety — how to manage it the week before
- How to apply for NCK licence after passing
- NCK exam centres in Kenya — full list and what to expect
- Higher Diploma NCK exam — Critical Care, Oncology, Renal breakdowns
- How to study for NCK while working as an intern
- NCK exam for foreign-trained nurses in Kenya
- Common pharmacology mistakes in NCK exams
